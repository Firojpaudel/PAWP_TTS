\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}\;
This paper presents \textbf{PAWP-TTS}, a compact non-autoregressive text-to-speech system for English, Nepali, and English--Nepali code-mixed speech at 24\,kHz, with no language model at inference. Deterministic pronunciation knowledge lives in a rule- and lexicon-based bilingual frontend that preserves native Nepali contrasts and avoids Hindi phonology, so the network only models acoustics. Counting every learned inference tensor, including the waveform decoder, \textbf{PAWP-1.5M} (1,490,390 parameters, 5.98\,MB) reaches 1.50\% English and 1.75\% Nepali character error rate under a single ASR recognizer on small held-out sets, and 5.04\% / 4.02\% Nepali / English phoneme error rate (PER) on 600 untouched test utterances. Because these metrics are partly circular, all systems are re-scored with two independent recognizers. Under these, synthetic speech is close to, but no better than, human recordings (English phone error 15.1\% vs.\ 13.4\%), and PAWP-1.5M reaches 18.1\% WER on a new 160-sentence code-mixed set. No listening test has been run yet. Decoder-centric \textbf{281K-parameter} models (1.1\,MB) remain intelligible, and at equal unique parameters a dense model has lower phone error than cross-layer basis sharing on all 6 seed$\times$language pairs under the independent recognizer ($p=0.01$). Fixing a frontend/label version mismatch removed a spurious Nepali substitution error entirely. The paper then studies \emph{weight-space programming}: inspection-guided, zero-training weight edits. Under strict controls (validation-only selection, one-shot test, paired bootstrap, matched random and same-parameter gradient baselines), edits show real selectivity (27:1 on word-boundary pauses with 96 weights) but no accuracy gain beyond controls in this exploratory study. Two apparent targets proved to be label or phonology artifacts. Code, ONNX graphs and weights are released.
