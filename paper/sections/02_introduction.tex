\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recent neural text-to-speech (TTS) systems increasingly rely on hundred-million to billion-parameter language models and neural audio codecs to achieve natural prosody and multilingual coverage. While effective in cloud deployments, these architectures incur high memory footprints, large time-to-first-audio (TTFA), and poor accessibility for low-resource languages such as Nepali---especially in edge, CPU-only, or browser-targeted settings.

Nepali presents specific phonological challenges that are routinely mishandled by generic Indic or Hindi-biased multilingual models~\cite{khatiwada2009nepali}: standard Kathmandu Nepali maintains a four-way stop laryngeal contrast, dental versus retroflex stops with post-vocalic flapping, alveolar affricates (/ts, ts$^h$, dz, dz$^h$/) rather than Hindi postalveolars, contrastive consonant gemination, an open-mid back unrounded inherent vowel rather than the Hindi central schwa, and an alveolar sibilant merger (/s/) across orthographic \textit{sa/\'{s}a/\d{s}a}. Moreover, everyday Nepali speech frequently code-switches mid-sentence with English.

This work investigates two research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Compact Bilingual Capacity:} How small can a complete, end-to-end 24\,kHz bilingual English--Nepali TTS system (including its neural waveform decoder) be made when deterministic linguistic knowledge is cleanly separated into a rule- and lexicon-backed frontend?
  \item \textbf{Diagnostic Weight-Space Programming (WSP):} Following the weight-space programming view that trained weights can be read and edited like a program~\cite{wsp2026}, can inspection-guided, zero-training edits (using rank-one tools such as ROME~\cite{meng2022rome}) surgically repair phonetic or timing defects in a compact TTS model without degrading the other language, and do such edits hold up against matched random and same-parameter gradient controls?
\end{enumerate}

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Open Compact Bilingual TTS at 1.5M and 281K Parameters:} Complete, audited parameter accounting (4-metric standard) across \texttt{PAWP-1.5M} (1,490,390 params, 5.98\,MB FP32) and \texttt{PAWP-300K} (281,078 unique / 328,158 materialized params, 1.12\,MB FP32) with language-gated low-rank residuals that fold statically into zero-overhead monolingual ONNX graphs.
  \item \textbf{Clean Frontend--Alignment Consistency:} Empirical demonstration that eliminating a frontend/cache version mismatch (\texttt{v2} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{v3}) nearly halves validation Nepali PER (7.06\% $\rightarrow$ 3.96\%) and eliminates a 55.1\% spurious \textit{va} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{/w/} artifact.
  \item \textbf{A Controlled Audit of Zero-Training WSP in Compact TTS:} Experiments with validation-only selection, one-shot tests and paired-bootstrap 95\% CIs show real selectivity (27:1 pause selectivity in 96 weights; zero cross-lingual collateral inside language residuals) but \emph{no accuracy gain beyond matched controls}, and identify three confounders that mimic model defects: cache-label drift, native phonology mismatch, and global duration bias.
\end{itemize}
